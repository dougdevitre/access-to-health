## National Health Statistics

### Leading Causes of Death (US, 2024)
1. Heart disease: ~683,000 deaths/year
2. Cancer: ~620,000 deaths/year
3. Accidents (unintentional injuries): ~197,000 deaths/year
4. Stroke: ~167,000 deaths/year
5. Chronic lower respiratory disease: ~146,000 deaths/year
6. Alzheimer's disease: ~116,000 deaths/year
7. Diabetes: ~94,000 deaths/year
8. Kidney disease: ~55,000 deaths/year
9. Chronic liver disease: ~52,000 deaths/year
10. Suicide: ~49,000 deaths/year (COVID-19 fell out of the top 10 in 2024)

### Health Disparities

**Life Expectancy** (2024)
- US average: ~79 years (verify current)
- White: ~79 years
- Black: ~75 years (gap narrowing but persistent)
- Hispanic: ~82 years (Hispanic paradox — despite lower income, longer life expectancy on average)
- Within metro areas: Up to 20-year gap between neighboring census tracts

**Maternal Mortality**
- US overall: ~18 per 100,000 live births in 2024 (highest among wealthy nations)
- Black women: ~45 per 100,000 (about 3x white women)
- White women: ~14 per 100,000
- 80% of maternal deaths are preventable

**Infant Mortality**
- US overall: ~5.5 per 1,000 live births (2024)
- Black: ~10.9 per 1,000 (2x overall rate)
- White: ~4.4 per 1,000
- Preterm birth rate: ~10% overall, ~14% Black

**Chronic Disease**
- Diabetes prevalence: ~11% of adults (37 million)
- Hypertension: ~47% of adults (120 million)
- Obesity: ~42% of adults
- Cancer: ~1.9 million new cases/year

**Mental Health**
- 1 in 5 adults experiences mental illness annually (~57 million)
- Suicide: ~49,000 deaths/year (10th leading cause overall, 2nd for ages 10-14)
- Only 46% of adults with mental illness receive treatment
- 988 Lifeline: 24/7 crisis support

**Substance Use**
- Overdose deaths: ~80,000 in 2024 (provisional; down about 27% from ~110,000 in 2023; fentanyl dominant)
- Alcohol use disorder: ~29 million adults
- Opioid use disorder: ~2.7 million adults
- Naloxone availability: Standing orders in most states

**Social Determinants**
- Food insecurity: ~48 million Americans (2024)
- Uninsured: ~27 million (7.9% of population, all of 2025)
- Homelessness: ~746,000 on a given night (January 2025 count)
- Poverty: ~10% of population (~34.5 million, 2025)

## Missouri-Specific Data

- Population: ~6.2 million
- Racial composition: ~80% white, ~12% Black, ~5% Hispanic, ~2% Asian, ~1% multiracial
- Median household income: ~$60,000
- Poverty rate: ~12-13%

### St. Louis Metropolitan Area
- Population: ~2.8 million (metro), ~290,000 (city)
- Delmar Divide: 18-year life expectancy gap across one street
- North St. Louis: Among highest infant mortality, STI, lead poisoning, violent crime rates nationally
- City-county separation (since 1876) creates jurisdictional fragmentation

### Missouri Health Infrastructure
- 115 local public health agencies
- Medicaid expanded 2021 (138% FPL)
- Naloxone standing order: Statewide
- 988 active statewide
- Good Samaritan law: Limited protection when calling 911 for an overdose. It can cover some charges, such as possession of drugs or paraphernalia and probation or parole violations. It does not cover active warrants, selling or making drugs, or other crimes (time2act.mo.gov/goodsamaritan)

## Data Storytelling Numbers

### Cost-Effectiveness Statistics (for advocacy)
- Every $1 invested in childhood immunization saves $10.90
- Every $1 invested in tobacco cessation saves $1.26 in healthcare costs within 1 year
- Every $1 invested in WIC saves $1.77-$3.13 in healthcare costs
- Community health workers produce $2.47 return for every $1 invested
- Lead poisoning prevention: $17-$221 return per $1 invested (depending on measure)
- Diabetes Prevention Program: $2,650 per QALY (well below cost-effectiveness threshold)
- Home visiting programs: $5.70 return per $1 invested (NFP model)

### Impact Numbers (for community presentations)
- 1 case of measles can infect 12-18 unvaccinated people
- 1 naloxone kit can reverse an overdose in 2-5 minutes
- 1 CHW can serve 50-100 clients per year
- 1 SDOH screening takes 5-15 minutes and can change a family's trajectory
- 1 smoke-free law reduces heart attacks by 15% within 1 year
- 1 WIC participant receives approximately $50/month in nutritious food

## Citation Rules

1. Always cite the source and year of any statistic
2. Use the most recent available data (note lag times — vital stats are typically 1-2 years behind)
3. For community presentations: Round to meaningful precision (say "about 1 in 5" not "19.7%")
4. Always provide context: "Higher than," "increasing since," "the gap has widened"
5. Disaggregate by race, ethnicity, income, and geography whenever possible (Guardrail #3)
6. Never present disparity data without naming structural causes
7. Never present disparity data without presenting your plan to address it
